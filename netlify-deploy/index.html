The whole book at a glance: 441 off-market accounting firms scored on how likely each owner is to sell, and the 60 buyers we'd take them to.

Reading the charts:

Seller-intent band
Our 0–100 readiness score, bucketed: High ≥ 70 · Medium 50–69 · Low < 50. Higher = more likely ready to sell.
Succession strength
How strong the retirement / no-successor signal is for the firm — the biggest driver of the score.
Owner career stage
Where the owner is in their working life. Late-career owners are the likeliest near-term sellers.
Outreach wave
Wave 1 = we already have a verified email (callable today). Wave 2 = needs a contact found first.
Revenue band
Firm size by revenue — drives which buyers are a fit.

The Call queue button filters to firms worth calling today: High or Medium intent, an email on file, not yet contacted. Service mix and industry niche are web-researched per firm; the intent score is unchanged. Edits save in this browser.

The demand side: 60 acquirers we can take sellers to. "Matched sellers" counts the firms where this buyer is a top-3, geographically-targeted fit (score ≥ 85) — not a national catch-all — so the number reflects real fit, not reach. Clicking a buyer opens its thesis and lets you track each approach (Approached → Interested → LOI) — a demand pipeline that mirrors the supply side.

The buyer types, in plain terms:

PE-Backed Consolidator
A platform already owned by private equity, actively rolling up smaller firms for scale. The most aggressive acquirers — move fast, pay up for size.
PE Platform
The anchor firm a PE sponsor buys first to build a roll-up around. Looking for a strong base to add to.
Top-100 Strategic
A large independent (national / super-regional) firm buying for talent, clients, and geography — strategic fit over financial engineering.
Regional Consolidator
A mid-size firm growing by acquiring nearby practices inside its own region.
Regional / Boutique Firm
A smaller independent making the occasional tuck-in acquisition.
Search Fund
An individual operator (often investor-backed) looking to buy and personally run one firm.
Independent Sponsor
A dealmaker who lines up an acquisition and raises the equity deal-by-deal, without a committed fund.
What the book is worth. Each opportunity carries an estimated enterprise value (revenue × a sector multiple) and a Manganelli fee, weighted by its stage win-probability. The cards separate live pipeline — deals actually in motion (Contacted and beyond) — from book potential, the untouched supply still sitting in Researched, so the weighted figure isn't mistaken for a committed forecast. Revenue is estimated, and the multiples, fees, and probabilities are tunable assumptions (see /docs/PIPELINE-STAGE-MODEL-SPEC.md); treat the dollars as directional.
Active deals — the firms actually in motion. A firm appears here once it reaches Mandated, has a buyer in outreach, or has an LOI, diligence item, or purchase-agreement issue logged. Open a firm to compare LOIs side-by-side, track diligence requests to a deadline and owner, and log purchase-agreement issues — all of it syncs across the team.

What this is

A prioritization engine for off-market accounting-firm M&A: a ranked book of sellers, a rolodex of buyers, and the matches between them — built to answer one question, "who do we contact first, and who would buy them?"

How the seller-intent score works

Each firm scores 0–100 from six transparent components, weighted toward the strongest seller-intent proxies: succession/retirement signal (max 35), owner age & tenure (25), ownership concentration (15), revenue/buyer-fit band (15), contactability (10), and a warm-path bonus (10, editable). High ≥ 70 · Medium 50–69 · Low < 50.

Data depth — what's populated vs. what needs a pass

Computed / reconciled (live now): seller-intent score & breakdown, succession strength, owner count & concentration, founder-led, revenue, founding year / years-in-business, buyer-fit tags, and named matched buyers — folded in from the underlying pipeline data.

Web-researched (done): service mix, industry niche, office count, and managing partner are now filled per firm for all 441 real firms from each firm's own website (one record is a placeholder test row). A few carry a short "verify" note where the source location or owner name didn't match the live site. Note: this pass updated the displayed facts only — it did not recompute the seller-intent scores or rankings.

Still needs a research pass or your CRM (left blank, never guessed): precise owner ages, exact partner tenure beyond what the signal states, and all warm-path/relationship fields (Joe/Sam/Running Numbers/known buyers). These are editable so you can fill them.

Service lines (Manganelli engagement types)

  • Sell-side mandate — win + run the sale to close.
  • Valuation / readiness assessment — paid pre-market analysis.
  • Post-close integration — support after the deal.
  • Buy-side search — reverse mandate for an acquirer.
  • Diligence packaging — prep a seller before going to market.
  • Earnout / seller-note administration — tracking after close.

Edits — stage, owner, activity log, warm-path, buyer-outreach status, next-actions — sync live across the team via a Supabase backend, so you and Joe always see the same book. A local copy is cached in your browser for offline use; the badge in the header shows whether you're synced or offline. (Same-firm simultaneous edits are last-write-wins at the firm level.) Access is gated by a shared team passphrase (Supabase Auth) — the shared edit/deal data is only readable or writable once you've unlocked.
